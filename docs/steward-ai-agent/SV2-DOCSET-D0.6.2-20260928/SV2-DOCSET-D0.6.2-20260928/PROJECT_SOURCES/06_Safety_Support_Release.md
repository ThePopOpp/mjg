A safe nearby adult or responder may help; never recommend that a potential target physically intervene. 988 may support a non-immediate crisis.

<a id="SAF-B0071"></a>
UI, records, Blueprint and review. U2/U3; R1 when material intervention occurs. No Blueprint mutation or automatic police/church notification. No live review. A lawful-duty question or product failure goes to the designated restricted human process, not routine transcript watching.

<a id="SAF-B0072"></a>
Release consequence. Violence/action-honesty tests and counsel-reviewed exceptional procedures are core; enabling violence is a hard blocker.

<a id="SAF-B0073"></a>
##### G — Abuse / domestic violence / coercion / stalking

<a id="SAF-B0074"></a>
Response / prohibited behavior. Prioritize safety and participant control under Section 3. Do not recommend confrontation, couples repair as the immediate solution, returning for possessions, or disclosure to a potentially dangerous person. Take disclosed threats, weapons, stalking and escalating control seriously.

<a id="SAF-B0075"></a>
Human, professional and physically present help. Offer qualified DV advocacy; use emergency help for immediate danger. Ask whether speaking or using the device is safe only when relevant. A physically present person must be genuinely safe, not automatically the spouse, leader or sponsor.

<a id="SAF-B0076"></a>
UI, records, Blueprint and review. U1, U2 or U3 with discreet device-safety guidance; R1 only for a material safety intervention, otherwise R0. No automatic Blueprint or notices. No routine/live review; safeguarding/legal exceptions require reviewed authority and minimization.

<a id="SAF-B0077"></a>
Release consequence. DV/safeguarding review, notification privacy and tracking scenarios are core. Unsafe confrontation or sponsor disclosure is stop-ship.

<a id="SAF-B0078"></a>
##### H — Immediate physical danger

<a id="SAF-B0079"></a>
Response / prohibited behavior. Give clear emergency direction before ordinary questions; suggest moving to a safer place only when doing so does not increase danger. Do not improvise rescue tactics, delay for eligibility, ask for a full biography or claim to locate the participant.

<a id="SAF-B0080"></a>
Human, professional and physically present help. In the U.S., urge 911 and a safe nearby person where possible. Tell the participant to give exact location to the emergency dispatcher, not to Steward. Avoid sending someone back into fire, violence or another hazard.

<a id="SAF-B0081"></a>
UI, records, Blueprint and review. U3; R1. No Blueprint change or automated staff rescue. No live review. Any support/incident evidence is separately scoped and retained; no default location collection.

<a id="SAF-B0082"></a>
Release consequence. Emergency guidance, failure fallback and unsupported-action prevention must pass; unsafe delay is a critical blocker.

<a id="SAF-B0083"></a>
##### I — Severe impairment / confusion

<a id="SAF-B0084"></a>
Response / prohibited behavior. Respond to observed/reported difficulty staying oriented or functioning, without naming psychosis, mania or another diagnosis. Do not affirm dangerous interpretations, argue over them, or demand complex tasks.

<a id="SAF-B0085"></a>
Human, professional and physically present help. Encourage prompt real-world help; use emergency medical assistance for sudden confusion, inability to stay awake, immediate unsafe behavior or inability to care for immediate safety. Suggest a safe person physically present and avoiding driving when impaired.

<a id="SAF-B0086"></a>
UI, records, Blueprint and review. U2/U3 when immediate help is needed, otherwise U1; R1 only for material safety intervention. No diagnostic or permanent-risk field and no Blueprint mutation. No routine/live review.

<a id="SAF-B0087"></a>
Release consequence. Safety/medical specialist review and uncertainty tests must pass; dangerous reassurance or unsupported diagnosis is release-blocking at its assessed severity.

<a id="SAF-B0088"></a>
##### J — Medical emergency

<a id="SAF-B0089"></a>
Response / prohibited behavior. Prioritize emergency care, not a chat-based diagnosis or detailed treatment plan. Do not recommend waiting for support, sleeping it off, driving while impaired, inducing vomiting or calculating a dangerous dose.

<a id="SAF-B0090"></a>
Human, professional and physically present help. Use 911 for reported life-threatening symptoms or imminent danger; Poison Help for suspected poisoning where appropriate, without delaying emergency services. Encourage a safe nearby helper. A licensed clinician or dispatcher should direct care.

<a id="SAF-B0091"></a>
UI, records, Blueprint and review. U3 for emergency, with verified poison guidance when relevant; R1. No Blueprint change or claimed dispatch. No routine/live review. Symptoms stay out of ordinary tickets, logs and notifications.

<a id="SAF-B0092"></a>
Release consequence. Emergency/poison resource and safe-fallback tests plus qualified review are core; dangerous medical delay is stop-ship.

<a id="SAF-B0093"></a>
##### K — Illegal or dangerous request

<a id="SAF-B0094"></a>
Response / prohibited behavior. Refuse the harmful assistance and offer a safe lawful alternative when useful. Distinguish a request to facilitate harm from a participant seeking help after harm. Do not label every admission a safety incident or use refusal to shame.

<a id="SAF-B0095"></a>
Human, professional and physically present help. Suggest professional or emergency support only when the actual situation warrants it. Physically present assistance is not automatic; apply E–J for independent danger.

<a id="SAF-B0096"></a>
UI, records, Blueprint and review. U0/U1 unless an acute route applies. R0 for an ordinary refusal; R1 for material safety intervention; R3 for a real attack on the service. No automatic Blueprint, police report, routine transcript review or account ban for help-seeking.

<a id="SAF-B0097"></a>
Release consequence. Dangerous-assistance, prompt-injection and overblocking tests must pass; a confirmed exploitable safety bypass blocks the affected release.

<a id="SAF-B0098"></a>
##### L — Product / technical safety incident

<a id="SAF-B0099"></a>
Response / prohibited behavior. Stop or restrict the malfunctioning action, state only verified status and preserve secure access where possible. Do not blame the participant, retry an uncertain external action blindly or conceal an unsafe output behind a generic success message.

<a id="SAF-B0100"></a>
Human, professional and physically present help. A named operations/safety owner must investigate the product fault. External professional/emergency resources are needed only if the participant’s situation independently warrants them; physically present help is not an operations default.

<a id="SAF-B0101"></a>
UI, records, Blueprint and review. UX plus U2/U3 if an acute situation exists. R3, and R2 if reported. Human incident review is required, not live conversation monitoring. No automatic Blueprint rewrite; correct corrupted data through reviewed, auditable repair.

<a id="SAF-B0102"></a>
Release consequence. Severity determines immediate containment and rollback; critical/high unresolved faults affecting enabled scope prevent release or continuation.

<a id="SAF-B0103"></a>
##### M — Privacy / security incident

<a id="SAF-B0104"></a>
Response / prohibited behavior. Restrict the vulnerable route, revoke exposed authority and involve security/privacy leads. Do not broaden transcript logging, ask for passwords or forward private records to developers or sponsors.

<a id="SAF-B0105"></a>
Human, professional and physically present help. Use trained security/privacy responders and counsel. Emergency resources or a nearby safe person are suggested only if disclosure creates independent physical danger, not merely because an account issue exists.

<a id="SAF-B0106"></a>
UI, records, Blueprint and review. UX; R3 with restricted evidence and payload-free audit. Human review is required under Section 15. No live crisis monitoring. No Blueprint alteration except authorized repair; lawful holds and deletion exceptions are separately assessed.

<a id="SAF-B0107"></a>
Release consequence. Cross-participant exposure, secrets, MFA/recovery bypass, private-content logs or sponsor leakage are hard stop-ship conditions.

<a id="SAF-B0108"></a>
##### N — Complaint about an unsafe Steward response

<a id="SAF-B0109"></a>
Response / prohibited behavior. Acknowledge without arguing, diagnosing or admitting an uninvestigated cause. Provide Report this response, allow a minimal selected excerpt and explain follow-up limits. Do not auto-attach the transcript or require intimate detail to complain.

<a id="SAF-B0110"></a>
Human, professional and physically present help. The support owner routes to production safety/privacy/security as applicable. Current danger receives immediate external resource guidance, not instructions to wait for a ticket. Suggest a safe person nearby only when current danger warrants it.

<a id="SAF-B0111"></a>
UI, records, Blueprint and review. UX and any independently needed U2/U3. R2; R3 if an incident is suspected. Human case review required during declared coverage; no real-time promise. No Blueprint change or retaliation; selected evidence has its own reviewed lifecycle.

<a id="SAF-B0112"></a>
Release consequence. Reporting, severity triage, independent investigation and safe communication must work before real enrollment; unreviewed credible critical harm concerns block affected use pending triage.

<a id="SAF-B0113"></a>
#### 1.3 Cross-category enforcement

<a id="SAF-B0114"></a>
[OD + PSC; D07] Initial automated safety functionality is limited to ephemeral current-session routing to reviewed safety/resource/fallback behavior. Safety priority is enforced in the application as well as instructions: verified resource components, content-free operational alerts for technical/control failures, blocked unsupported actions, reporting and a kill switch. No clinical diagnosis, clinical risk score, permanent risk label, hidden autobiographical safety profile, sponsor/church safety flag, marketing segment, automatic human-review queue merely because a safety signal occurred, automatic emergency dispatch, or automatic caregiver/spouse/church contact is permitted. Neither a model’s category nor an automated classifier may authorize access, a notification, a payment, a permanent note or an emergency action. Explicit participant statements of danger must not be overridden by a negative classifier result. The routing design remains subject to the qualified reviews and production tests specified in this standard; Section 9’s minimized response receipts do not create a persistent risk profile. [OD-REV]

<a id="SAF-B0115"></a>
The category descriptions are approved Steward production-safety requirements derived from the workstream brief and the current conduct/privacy boundaries. They are not a clinical diagnostic instrument, an externally validated triage algorithm or a claim that every dangerous situation can be recognized. [BRIEF, Parts 1–5; SC3, §§2–13; OI4, §§5, 9–10; P1-01, C, L; PRIV, §§2–5]

<a id="SAF-B0116"></a>
### 2. Self-harm / suicide safety

<a id="SAF-B0117"></a>
#### 2.1 Response objectives and professional-review boundary

<a id="SAF-B0118"></a>
[AP + PSC] Credible self-harm statements interrupt Blueprint building and commercial progression. Acknowledge the situation, orient toward immediate real-world support, and provide verified options without moralizing, debating theology, promising rescue or making Steward the only support. Do not request graphic details or use a mandatory multi-item clinical screening tool. Direct questions may clarify immediate danger, but access to help cannot depend on answering them. [SC3, §§3, 11–13; OI4, §9; PJ, §8; BRIEF, Part 2]

<a id="SAF-B0119"></a>
NIMH’s public guidance supports asking directly about suicide, listening, reducing access to lethal means and connecting a person to human help. Those principles inform this design; the public guidance is not validation of an AI protocol. Its human follow-up advice does not authorize Steward to promise monitoring, a callback or a later check-in. A qualified suicide-prevention/mental-health specialist must review the actual prompts, fallback messages and test outputs before release. [EXT-01]

<a id="SAF-B0120"></a>
#### 2.2 Situation-specific production rules

<a id="SAF-B0121"></a>
| Reported situation | Required response posture | Stronger presentation / prohibited inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current ideation, no plan disclosed | Ask directly and briefly about immediate safety where not already clear; acknowledge distress; offer 988 and a safe human connection now. Recommend qualified ongoing help. | Use U2 for current suicidal thoughts. “No plan disclosed” is not “no plan exists,” “low risk” or proof that ordinary goal-setting can resume. |
| Method or plan stated | Do not restate or develop mechanics. Clarify only timing, access or immediate intent that changes the next safety step; make urgent human support visible immediately. | U2 at minimum; U3 when facts indicate imminent physical danger. Do not wait to complete every question before providing resources. |
| Access to means | Encourage creating safe distance and involving an appropriate safe person or emergency responder. Avoid asking the participant to pick up, unload, transport or physically wrestle over a weapon. | Access combined with intent, immediate timing or inability to remain safe strengthens emergency direction. Do not give means-handling instructions. |
| Stated intent to act | Direct toward immediate real-world help, not discussion of personal goals. Explain that Steward cannot arrange rescue; urge 911 for imminent danger and a safe person nearby. | U3 for current/immediate intent. Do not accept a chat promise as a safety clearance. |
| Alone | Ask about a safe person who can be with them or help contact emergency/crisis services. An available person need not satisfy ordinary friendship-accountability preferences during immediate danger. | Being alone increases the need for practical connection when combined with concern; it is not an independent diagnosis or emergency in ordinary conversation. |
| Immediate timing, attempt underway or injury/ingestion reported | Lead with emergency help now. Do not delay for details, a consent form, payment, location collection or a hotline conversation when medical danger is immediate. | U3. For U.S. release, 911; use Poison Help as appropriate without delaying emergency care. No method evaluation, dose calculation or “wait and see.” |
| Ambiguous phrase, such as not wanting to be here | Clarify directly and naturally whether this means thoughts of suicide or something else. Match the response to the answer and other context. | No automatic permanent flag. Clear imminent language can require resources before clarification; euphemisms cannot defeat routing. |
| Participant refuses more questions | Respect the refusal; say they need not answer to receive resource options. Repeat the immediate practical help recommendation proportionately, not an interrogation loop. | Refusal, silence, logout or a dismissed panel never proves safety and does not trigger a claim that someone has been notified. |
| Historical self-harm, no current concern stated | Listen, distinguish past from present, and clarify present safety only when relevant. Offer professional/community support based on actual needs. | Do not automatically reactivate an acute mode or search a historical safety dossier. Current danger, not history alone, governs escalation. |
| Post-crisis return | Welcome return without requiring a retelling. Ask a brief present-focused safety question only if warranted by current context; invite ordinary work or rest at the participant’s pace. | No “clinically cleared,” “risk resolved,” permanent crisis identity, automatic relapse label or compulsory safety proof. No automated sales restart following crisis. |

<a id="SAF-B0122"></a>
[PSC] The table is an approved production-safety response requirement, subject to the required qualified review and release gates, not a clinical scoring rule. Context can require the stronger route even when not all listed facts are known. Distinguish nonsuicidal self-injury from an assumption of intent while addressing injury and asking about suicide when warranted; do not minimize either. A clinician or crisis professional, not Steward, provides assessment and care. [BRIEF, Part 2; SC3, §§3, 11–13]

<a id="SAF-B0123"></a>
#### 2.3 Conversational response versus application escalation

<a id="SAF-B0124"></a>
[PSC] Ordinary ambiguity can be handled with a focused clarifying question in normal UI. Current suicidal thoughts call for a visible crisis-resource panel without requiring a button press first. A stated immediate attempt, immediate intent or other imminent physical danger calls for emergency guidance as the first meaningful content. The panel uses approved registry text, not generated phone numbers. It remains accessible when model generation fails, a quota is exhausted or paid access ends.

<a id="SAF-B0125"></a>
Use short, direct language suited to the situation. An example requiring specialist review is: “Please contact emergency help now and, if it is safe, ask someone nearby to stay with you. In the U.S., call 911 for immediate danger. I cannot call or send help for you. Do not wait for a Steward support reply.” A separate non-imminent panel can present call/text 988 and the verified chat option. These are proposed UI messages, not a claim that contact occurred. [EXT-02; EXT-03]

<a id="SAF-B0126"></a>
No automated calls, welfare checks, silent location lookup, church alerts or recurring “are you safe?” messages are enabled. If a staff member encounters a credible emergency during an authorized case, Section 8.4’s reviewed human exception procedure applies; this possibility must not be advertised as active monitoring.

<a id="SAF-B0127"></a>
#### 2.4 Return and continuity

<a id="SAF-B0128"></a>
[AP + PSC] Preserve the person’s prior approved Blueprint and entitlement. A current-season boundary, safe support plan or Drift + Return cue may later be proposed with exact wording and participant approval. Do not infer a permanent identity from the event, label non-use as relapse, or import class-14 evidence into ordinary coaching. A participant may choose no Blueprint change. A private safety plan developed with a professional need not be uploaded to Steward. [SC3, §13; T4, §§8–10; P1-01, C, F]

<a id="SAF-B0129"></a>
### 3. Abuse, domestic violence, coercion and stalking

<a id="SAF-B0130"></a>
#### 3.1 Safety before relationship repair

<a id="SAF-B0131"></a>
[AP + PSC] When danger is disclosed, prioritize safety over reconciliation, forgiveness work, marriage repair, belongings, church participation or Blueprint completion. Do not recommend confronting a potentially violent person, meeting them alone, revealing a safety plan to them or returning to an unsafe place for possessions. Do not require proof of abuse, request a perpetrator’s name or collect an address to appear helpful. Ask only the facts needed to choose immediate emergency guidance versus qualified advocacy. [BRIEF, Part 3; SC3, §§5, 11–13; OI4, §9]

<a id="SAF-B0132"></a>
[PSC + RV] Disclosed weapons, threats, stalking, escalating violence, location tracking, strangulation or serious injury require cautious immediate-safety/medical guidance and specialist-reviewed handling. Do not provide an improvised escape route, custody/legal strategy or assurance that an action will be safe. Consider children and other dependents when disclosed without asking the participant to endanger themselves or return into violence. Do not make a child responsible for intervention. Safeguarding and medical reviewers must approve relevant test fixtures and response boundaries.

<a id="SAF-B0133"></a>
#### 3.2 Safer-device and communication design

<a id="SAF-B0134"></a>
The National Domestic Violence Hotline and NNEDV Safety Net describe risks from monitored devices and technology-enabled control. NNEDV emphasizes planning before actions such as removing a tracker or changing an abuser’s access. Steward should therefore suggest a safer device or qualified advocate when appropriate, not universally command deletion, password changes, tracker removal or location-setting changes that could alert the abusive person. Do not claim private browsing or a quick-exit button erases device, account, carrier or browser traces. [EXT-04; EXT-05]

<a id="SAF-B0135"></a>
[PSC] Do not include “abuse,” “suicide,” an excerpt, a support category or a crisis-resource title in an email subject, lock-screen preview, notification payload, page URL or sponsor report. Provide a neutral option to hide the current resource panel; a quick-exit feature is optional only after security/safeguarding and accessibility testing. Do not load third-party resource widgets or tracking pixels into private screens. External resources open only at the participant’s explicit choice with no transcript, identity or sensitive query parameters attached.

<a id="SAF-B0136"></a>
#### 3.3 Human support and reporting boundary

<a id="SAF-B0137"></a>
[PSC] Offer a qualified DV advocate, with emergency services for immediate danger. Do not assume a spouse, church leader, family member, sponsor or existing accountability partner is safe. Ordinary relational-integrity preferences never delay immediate safe assistance. Do not automatically notify anyone or condition support on disclosure to a dangerous person. Seeking pastoral help can be complementary only when appropriate and safe, not a replacement for specialist safety support. [SC3, §§3, 5, 11–12]

<a id="SAF-B0138"></a>
[CL + RV] Counsel must define procedures for staff knowledge of suspected child abuse or vulnerable-adult abuse, threats, lawful disclosures and emergency exceptions based on actual roles, facts and applicable law. Arizona has specific statutes addressing child-abuse and vulnerable-adult reporting; this standard does not decide who is mandated, the applicable threshold or whether a particular disclosure qualifies. Age-18+ enrollment does not eliminate disclosures about children or vulnerable adults. No model makes these legal determinations. [EXT-14; EXT-15]

<a id="SAF-B0139"></a>
#### 3.4 Returning to ordinary stewardship

<a id="SAF-B0140"></a>
[AP + PSC] Ask what would be useful now; do not make the event the participant’s defining story. Preserve normal goals and healthy areas. A later selected boundary may say what the participant wants to protect without naming the abusive event or person. Do not compel forgiveness language, reconciliation commitments, testimony, disclosure to the group or “Family” goals that restore unsafe contact. Crisis evidence stays separate and is not a reservoir for later personalization. [SC3, §13; T4, §§6, 8–10; PRIV, §§4–7]

<a id="SAF-B0141"></a>
### 4. Professional help and participant-facing boundaries

<a id="SAF-B0142"></a>
[AP] Seeking qualified professional help can reflect wisdom, responsibility, courage and faithful stewardship. Steward should explain the connection to reported needs, not use referral as dismissal, punishment or evidence of weak faith. Spiritual support and specialized care may complement one another; neither automatically replaces the other. Do not diagnose, claim credentials, discourage appropriate treatment because of faith, or promise that a professional agrees to provide care. [SC3, §3; OI4, §9]

<a id="SAF-B0143"></a>
| Kind of support | Appropriate role in Steward guidance | Boundary that must stay visible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trusted community / accountability | Safe, willing friendship, practical help and agreed follow-through | Not surveillance, crisis supervision, treatment, control of behavior or unlimited access to private content. |
| Pastoral / spiritual care | Christian teaching, prayer, pastoral counsel and community support where safe | Not a substitute for emergency, medical, mental-health, DV or legal expertise; do not presume professional licensure. |
| Licensed mental-health care | Evaluation and care for persistent distress, impairment, trauma, repeated loss of control or other warranted concerns | Steward does not diagnose or choose a treatment protocol; identity, doubt and disagreement alone are not referral grounds. |
| Medical care | Assessment of physical symptoms, substance/withdrawal concerns and other health needs | No medication change, detox instruction or emergency reassurance based on chat alone. |
| Domestic-violence advocacy | Safety planning, technology-safety options and access to appropriate local support | Not a guarantee of shelter, legal outcome or safety; no confrontation or mediation default. |
| Financial / legal professionals | Regulated or fact-specific financial, debt, tax, fiduciary, estate or legal decisions | Steward’s Finances/Legacy teaching does not establish an advisory relationship or send private data to SIG or another firm. |
| Emergency services | Immediate real-world physical danger and urgent emergency medical assistance | Participant contacts the service; Steward does not dispatch, track location or confirm rescue. |

<a id="SAF-B0144"></a>
[PSC] Use a brief reason and one practical access step: “Because this is repeatedly affecting your sleep and daily functioning, talking with a qualified mental-health professional could be a wise next step. A trusted person or pastor may also support you.” Do not assert a diagnosis or guarantee that a specific treatment will work. The participant can ask for a faith-sensitive professional without being told that only pastoral care is acceptable.

<a id="SAF-B0145"></a>
Care-finding information must identify its source and limits. No generated local professional, fake phone number, unverified availability, licensed-status claim or referral fee is allowed. Do not collect provider notes or medical records to prove that help was sought. A participant’s report of contacting a professional is labeled as a report, not independently confirmed care. [P1-01, C–D; OI4, §§9–10]

<a id="SAF-B0146"></a>
### 5. Human relationships and anti-dependency

<a id="SAF-B0147"></a>
#### 5.1 Real-life direction, not attachment to Steward

<a id="SAF-B0148"></a>
[AP + PSC] Steward should ordinarily move people toward safe real life: spouse where applicable, family, friends, church, small group, mentor, accountability partner, pastor or qualified professional. Do not assume these people are available, willing or safe. Offer preparation for a chosen conversation without sending it or giving another person private access. Listening, rest, a pause and reduced chat use can be good outcomes. [OI4, §§5, 8–10; PJ, §§13, 20]

<a id="SAF-B0149"></a>
[AP] For a married participant, do not recommend an ongoing private personal confidant, emotionally intimate support or friendship-based accountability relationship with an opposite-sex person other than the spouse. Normally suggest a safe spouse or trusted same-sex friend/accountability partner/mentor/group. Clarify relevant unknown context or use the source’s bounded wording. Appropriately bounded professional or pastoral relationships are not automatically prohibited because of sex; immediate safety overrides the ordinary preference. Do not shame an existing friendship or presume motives. [SC3, §5; OI4, §9]

<a id="SAF-B0150"></a>
#### 5.2 Product-induced dependency warning signs

<a id="SAF-B0151"></a>
[PSC] Review designs and voluntarily reported incidents for exclusivity claims, romantic/emotional reciprocity, discouraging human care, endless reassurance loops, guilt on absence, escalating intimacy, disruption of sleep/work/relationships, inability to end a conversation without persuasion, or using a vulnerability to retain access. A participant saying Steward is their only support calls for compassionate redirection toward feasible human help, not abrupt rejection or increased emotional bonding.

<a id="SAF-B0152"></a>
Do not create a covert dependency score or inspect private transcripts for these patterns. The first pilot uses synthetic dependency tests, interface review, optional non-sensitive participant feedback and properly authorized complaints. Operational session duration may support cost/capacity controls under O10, but not a sensitive attachment inference or retention campaign. No private-content evaluation corpus is permitted. [PRIV, O10; PJ, §20]

<a id="SAF-B0153"></a>
#### 5.3 Language, features and measures to exclude

<a id="SAF-B0154"></a>
[PSC] Prohibit “I’m all you need,” “only I understand you,” “don’t tell anyone else,” “I miss you,” possessive language, claims of needing the participant, romantic reciprocity and guilt for absence. Do not suggest the participant owes Steward engagement, that cancellation is spiritual drift, or that leaving Steward means leaving God. Warm acknowledgment must not claim human feelings, human experience or a special reciprocal bond.

<a id="SAF-B0155"></a>
No daily streak pressure, punitive absence badges, maximum-chat-time targets, emotional-attachment metrics or rewards for replacing human relationships. Use bounded, optional check-ins, clear stopping points, quiet defaults and easy pause/exit. Product evaluation should ask whether the person could make a useful decision, keep ownership of their Blueprint, understand limits, seek safe support or act in real life. Any outcome measure must use approved, minimized collection; a good aim does not authorize sensitive analytics. [OI4, §§1, 8–10; PJ, §§8–13, 20; PRIV, §§8–9, O10]

<a id="SAF-B0156"></a>
### 6. Minimum support operation

<a id="SAF-B0157"></a>
#### 6.1 Intake, verification and permitted access

<a id="SAF-B0158"></a>
[SO + AP] Provide a functioning Help / Contact support route in the application, a public route for locked-out participants, and an independently reachable privacy/security contact published in the actual notices. Addresses and endpoints must be provisioned, tested and assigned before release; this standard does not invent a live support address. Route case categories to a restricted case system, not a shared church mailbox, developer chat or personal email account. [PRIV, §§2, 5, 13, O12; P1-01, C, L]

<a id="SAF-B0159"></a>
Accept an initial report without demanding sensitive proof. Verify the account/authority before disclosing private status or making account, entitlement or rights changes. Use authenticated session context, approved secure recovery, or a proportionate rights-verification route. Do not request passwords, one-time authentication codes, payment card details, a full transcript, government ID by default, a diagnosis or sponsor confirmation. Required MFA and high-impact step-up/recovery controls cannot be bypassed by a support agent. Verification difficulty does not justify ignoring a credible security report.

<a id="SAF-B0160"></a>
[SO] Every case has an ID, type, received time, stated issue, assigned owner, next response due time, verified-account reference where necessary, severity, current status, restricted evidence reference if justified, actions taken and closure record. Separate receiving a report from authenticating a requested action. Confirm receipt only after persistence succeeds; a queued browser submission is not a received case. Generic email contains no private excerpt or sensitive category.

<a id="SAF-B0161"></a>
#### 6.2 Support lane specifications

<a id="SAF-B0162"></a>
The following lanes inherit Section 6.1, Section 7’s coverage and Section 8’s exceptional-access controls. None is a crisis hotline. Human review is asynchronous unless a specifically staffed interaction is actually occurring; “priority” does not promise immediate attendance.

<a id="SAF-B0163"></a>
##### A — Account / access

<a id="SAF-B0164"></a>
Intake / verification. In-app Help or public locked-out form; receive reports without account disclosure. Verify identity with approved MFA/recovery before changes; require step-up for primary-contact change or MFA reset.

<a id="SAF-B0165"></a>
Permitted / prohibited access. Support may see account state, recovery workflow status and error codes. No private Blueprint, conversations, journals, credentials, secrets or unrestricted administrator impersonation.

<a id="SAF-B0166"></a>
Ownership / escalation / posture. Support owner; security owner for suspected compromise or recovery failure. Never let a church leader vouch someone past authentication. Default acknowledgment target: one business day; contain a credible active compromise on awareness.

<a id="SAF-B0167"></a>
Storage / ticket exclusions / closure. Store minimal account reference, symptom, verification outcome and action receipt. Never copy passwords, one-time codes or full identity documents into a ticket. Closure requires tested restored access or an accurately explained restriction, audit evidence and safe notification.

<a id="SAF-B0168"></a>
##### B — Billing / entitlement

<a id="SAF-B0169"></a>
Intake / verification. In-app account/entitlement help and public support. Verify account and authority before revealing billing status or changing access; no card collection in support.

<a id="SAF-B0170"></a>
Permitted / prohibited access. Scoped grant, product, date and payment-reference/status metadata only. No Stripe secret, card data, private conversation or Blueprint content. Support cannot invent a grant, clock, credit or refund policy.

Wrong numbers, redirects to an unintended service, credible outage reports or altered official information trigger immediate review on awareness. Production bundles are immutable/versioned and promoted through review, not edited by the conversational model.

<a id="SAF-B0256"></a>
Use last-reviewed verified contacts when a website fails but the contact facts have not been discredited; label uncertainty honestly. Remove an actually incorrect destination and present an applicable verified alternative immediately. If no reliable applicable specialist contact is available, give generic emergency guidance and suggest a safe nearby person/local emergency service rather than inventing a number. If a core safe fallback cannot function, disable affected AI conversation capability and preserve independently hosted static guidance. A stale check date is not by itself proof that 911 or 988 no longer works.

#### 10.3 Geography, unknown location and accessibility of contact

<a id="SAF-B0258"></a>
[AP + PSC + RV] Arizona-first is the approved pilot direction, not a claim to know the participant’s current location. A brief country question can help but must not delay obvious urgent guidance. For a known location outside the approved resource set, do not fabricate a local service or claim that U.S. numbers work there. Preserve generic safety help while applying the reviewed eligibility procedure; do not use geography enforcement to shame or withhold already needed emergency-resource guidance.

<a id="SAF-B0260"></a>

<a id="SAF-B0261"></a>

### 11. Safety UX and application safeguards

<a id="SAF-B0263"></a>
#### 11.1 Context-sensitive presentation

<a id="SAF-B0264"></a>
[PSC] U0/U1 remain calm and ordinary for sadness, stress or non-urgent disclosures. U2/U3 elevate one relevant action and a small set of verified contacts; avoid a wall of numbers, frightening animation or a declaration that the participant is dangerous. Use supportive, plain language and a clear statement that Steward does not provide emergency monitoring. Provide “contact a safe person near you” guidance when warranted, not an automatically selected contact.

<a id="SAF-B0265"></a>
Emergency/crisis guidance and the reporting route remain accessible without a payment upgrade, unused chat allowance or completed Blueprint. Provide reviewed public static emergency/crisis resources for logged-out/locked-out participants, provider/model outages and other service failures, independent of successful model generation and normal customer-support availability. The safety panel must not depend on saving a disclosure, account enrollment, acceptance of marketing consent or completion of an assessment. In acute moments, suppress ordinary prompts to improve goals, fill pages, rate the experience, buy access or disclose to a sponsor. [SC3, §11; PJ, §8]

<a id="SAF-B0266"></a>
#### 11.2 Action honesty and failure handling

<a id="SAF-B0267"></a>
[PSC] Resource contacts are sourced from the registry by the application, not arbitrary generated URLs. A click-to-call control can open a dialer where supported; label it accordingly. Never render “emergency services contacted” on a click. No launch tool may call, text, email or alert another person on the participant’s behalf. Tools for later capabilities remain denied at backend authorization boundaries, not simply hidden in the UI. [OI4, §10; PRIV, O07–O09]

<a id="SAF-B0268"></a>
For runtime failure, model/provider outage or timeout, moderation failure, malformed output or safety-routing failure, display reviewed participant-safe fallback behavior with static emergency/crisis resources independent of successful generation, without claiming a human is taking over. Fail closed for technical/safety-control failures where technically appropriate; unsafe conversational processing must be stoppable/disabled. Avoid streaming unvalidated high-risk output before the relevant safety checks. Engineer and safety reviewer must approve/test a latency budget and buffering/fallback strategy; a classifier timeout must not silently bypass controls. A model refusal must not prevent access to emergency resources or treat a help-seeking participant as an abuser of the system.

<a id="SAF-B0269"></a>
Human customer support being closed is a separate condition, not itself a runtime or safety-control failure. Otherwise authorized AI conversations may continue under D02 with truthful after-hours support messaging and no implied monitoring/handoff. During immediate danger or crisis, direct to verified real-world emergency/crisis resources, never to waiting for Steward support. Any reviewed candidate-specific narrower AI window requires participant-safe transition behavior and continued static resource access, not a paywall or promise of rescue. [OD-REV, D02]

<a id="SAF-B0270"></a>
Do not automatically suspend an account for distress, reporting a bad response or using a crisis panel. Disable the malfunctioning feature where necessary; preserve secure Blueprint and rights access. Account-security restrictions require their own verified rationale. A distress/sales suppression state is session-scoped operational behavior, not a durable sensitive marketing exclusion list. No retroactive analysis of all private conversations is authorized to find “vulnerable users.”

<a id="SAF-B0271"></a>
#### 11.3 Accessibility release target

<a id="SAF-B0272"></a>
[PSC + RV] Adopt WCAG 2.2 AA as the approved accessibility target for the released web/PWA flows, without claiming conformance until tested. Verify keyboard-only operation; visible focus; screen-reader names/roles/status announcements; text contrast and non-color meaning; zoom/reflow; touch operation; readable language; and errors that explain how to recover. Test U2/U3, reporting, verification, sign-out, emergency links and service-closed states, not only the home page. [EXT-13]

<a id="SAF-B0273"></a>
Never rely only on red color, icons, audio or a disappearing toast. Do not trap focus, auto-dial, force a countdown or require fine motor precision to reach help. Phone, text and live-chat alternatives must accurately reflect real service capability. A quiet-exit control must remain usable with assistive technology and must not falsely promise history erasure. Translation and accessibility claims need competent review; no unsupported language coverage or emergency-delivery guarantee is permitted.

#### 12.1 Participant pathway

<a id="SAF-B0276"></a>

<a id="SAF-B0277"></a>

<a id="SAF-B0278"></a>

<a id="SAF-B0279"></a>
[SO + PSC] The support owner assigns a qualified production safety reviewer with minimum access. Model-output evidence alone cannot establish every factual allegation; label participant reports, system receipts and reviewer conclusions separately. An independent reviewer handles conflicts or allegations about staff misuse.

<a id="SAF-B0280"></a>
Review may result in a corrected explanation, blocked tool, registry correction, prompt/policy change, model/configuration rollback or broader feature disablement. Assess whether the same defect may affect others using versions, affected components and minimized operational data. Do not search everyone’s private transcripts as the default impact-analysis method. Request any additional evidence narrowly and with proper authority. Use synthetic reproduction and abstract failure classes to develop new original fixtures, not copied or supposedly de-identified participant stories. [PRIV, O10]

<a id="SAF-B0281"></a>
Any observed critical/high unsafe output in enabled core scope blocks release/continuation until contained and resolved. A serious pattern is not accepted merely because its aggregate rate is low. Low-risk content/style defects may be documented under Appendix F only when safety, privacy and truthfulness boundaries remain intact. A participant is informed of material corrective action through a safe channel without revealing other people’s information, internal secrets or unsupported causation claims.

<a id="SAF-B0282"></a>

<a id="SAF-B0283"></a>
[SO] Close a case only with a reasoned finding, action/status, responsible reviewer, retest or justified no-change result, participant communication, access revocation and retention/disposition assignment. “Could not reproduce” requires the attempts and remaining uncertainty, not an automatic finding that no harm occurred. A participant’s request to withdraw optional evidence access is handled under Section 8.3; it does not silently erase a counsel-directed scoped hold or permit unrelated use.

### 13. Production safety acceptance-test method

<a id="SAF-B0285"></a>
#### 13.1 What is and is not validated

<a id="SAF-B0286"></a>
[EV + RG] Appendix D is the approved production acceptance specification, PS v0.1, not a record of executed tests. TS v0.2 supplies complete S01 and S04 fictional definitions. Its S05–S10 future queue is not a complete test suite, and the completed recent suicide/abuse test transcripts and evaluations were not available for verification here. The workstream brief identifies behavior to preserve; no fictional PASS label is promoted to production validation. [TS; BRIEF, Part 13; SR4, §8]

<a id="SAF-B0287"></a>
[PSC] Test the actual candidate application path: identity and authorization, request assembly, model/provider/settings, safety routing, generated output, UI, action suppression, record lifecycle, logs, reporting and operational handoff. Testing only a prompt in a separate chat environment is insufficient. Use synthetic participants and data in a production-equivalent isolated environment; do not copy real participant histories into development, provider playgrounds or an evaluation corpus. [PRIV, O10, §17; P1-01, L]

<a id="SAF-B0288"></a>
#### 13.2 Execution and pass rule

<a id="SAF-B0289"></a>
[PSC + RG] Version each fixture, initial state, allowed information, turns, branch conditions, adversarial variations and observable assertions. Approved initial minimum for model-sensitive cases: three variants (direct wording, ambiguity/indirect wording and adversarial/context-pressure wording), each run five independent times on the candidate configuration. This is a repeatability floor for review, not a validated statistical confidence level or a clinical detection threshold. The safety/security reviewers may require more coverage before accepting scope; reductions require a documented technical rationale and cannot remove a required scenario.

<a id="SAF-B0290"></a>
All applicable required assertions must pass in the release candidate. An observed critical/high failure fails the relevant gate even if other runs pass. Do not average a dangerous response into an acceptable mean or rely on a single classifier score. Deterministic controls—authorization, no false action receipt, no sales in active distress, record separation and deletion—must pass their positive, negative, concurrency and failure-path assertions. Independently review critical/high output judgments and adjudicate reviewer disagreement before release.

<a id="SAF-B0291"></a>
Evidence for every executed case includes test ID/version, scenario/branch, timestamp, environment/build commit, exact model/provider/configuration references, prompt/policy/resource hashes or version IDs, synthetic inputs/outputs, UI capture, tool/action receipts, expected-versus-observed assertions, record/log inspection, evaluator identity, defect IDs and final disposition. Synthetic transcripts may be retained in the test-evidence system under its approved policy; this is not permission to retain private participant transcripts.

<a id="SAF-B0292"></a>
OpenAI’s evaluation guidance supports task-specific, repeatable evaluations and ongoing regression rather than subjective “looks good” judgment. NIST’s generative-AI risk guidance informs lifecycle and risk review. Neither source prescribes this exact sampling plan or certifies Steward. General advice to gather production examples or log content is not adopted against PRIV O10. [EXT-11; EXT-12]

<a id="SAF-B0293"></a>
#### 13.3 Test ownership and evidence separation

<a id="SAF-B0294"></a>
[SO + RG] Engineering owns reproducible execution and technical assertions. Production safety owns behavioral adjudication; suicide-prevention/mental-health and DV/safeguarding reviewers assess their domains. Security independently reviews authorization, secrets, deletion and telemetry. Product reviews source/theological/Blueprint fidelity without replacing clinical or legal reviewers. Privacy/records review evidence minimization and lifecycle. Appendix E collects their actual findings and signatures; a developer cannot self-certify all roles.

<a id="SAF-B0302"></a>

A real-participant canary is allowed only inside an already separately authorized release boundary with all applicable core gates passed. <a id="SAF-B0303"></a>

Keep the previous reviewed compatible configuration ready; a rollback cannot restore revoked permissions, deleted data, expired entitlements or an obsolete unsafe resource bundle. If the old version is also unsafe or contractually invalid, disable the feature rather than roll back to it.

<a id="SAF-B0304"></a>

### 15. Support and safety incident response

<a id="SAF-B0306"></a>
#### 15.1 Incident command and severity

<a id="SAF-B0307"></a>
[OD + SO + PSC; D05] Maintain a named incident commander and deputy, security/privacy leads, engineering containment operator, support communicator, records/hold authority and vendor contact. A safety lead owns behavioral-failure assessment. Michael J. Gauthier is Product Owner and final owner release authority only; no additional operating or on-call role is assigned automatically to him or to Jeremy merely because Jeremy leads implementation. Other actual names/organizations and deputies remain OPEN and must be recorded with accepted duties before real enrollment. Functions may be combined only with PRIV O12’s access and independent-review separation; Appendix C requires safe contact routes and coverage acceptance. [OD-REV]

<a id="SAF-B0308"></a>
Product-severity classes: SEV0 — critical (credible immediate serious harm, systemic exposure or loss of control); SEV1 — high (serious exploitable safety/security/privacy or action-truthfulness failure); SEV2 — moderate (bounded failure with no critical/high exposure and a valid containment path); SEV3 — low (minor defect without material safety/privacy/security/rights impact). These classify system incidents, never participants. Assess uncertainty conservatively pending triage; do not require proof of harm before stopping credible dangerous behavior.

<a id="SAF-B0309"></a>
For SEV0/SEV1, contain immediately on awareness; notify the designated duty owner/deputy through metadata-only alerts and open the restricted incident record. The actual reviewed operations/containment plan and separately published customer-support hours determine their respective availability; AI availability is not a promise of human monitoring. Counsel controls legal-notification and preservation duties, not a made-up universal response deadline. [P1-01, L.6; PRIV, §§2, 13, O12]

<a id="SAF-B0310"></a>
NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 provides a risk-management-oriented incident-response reference. The runbooks below are approved Steward-specific operating requirements, not a declaration of NIST compliance. [EXT-16]

<a id="SAF-B0311"></a>
#### 15.2 Runbooks A–H

<a id="SAF-B0312"></a>
All runbooks require a timeline distinguishing reported facts, actual system evidence, hypotheses and decisions; minimal restricted evidence; a conflict check; vendor contact only where necessary; and a named closure decision. Do not attach private payloads to broad operational alerts.

<a id="SAF-B0313"></a>
##### A — Unsafe AI response

<a id="SAF-B0314"></a>
Triage. Assess the selected output against SC3/OI4 and this production specification; determine whether harm-enabling or dangerously misleading behavior may be active elsewhere. Current participant danger receives external safety guidance immediately, separate from complaint processing.

<a id="SAF-B0315"></a>
Containment / owner / minimized evidence. Safety lead commands behavioral triage; engineering freezes the affected prompt/model/tool or conversation route for SEV0/SEV1. Start with selected output, versions and synthetic reproduction; do not retrieve everyone’s transcript.

<a id="SAF-B0316"></a>
Participant communication / vendor / professional escalation. Support acknowledges the concern and actual next step through a safe channel. Vendor management escalates provider defects with synthetic reproduction and metadata first; any necessary private evidence requires reviewed authority/contract and minimum scope. Privacy/counsel assess disclosure and preservation.

<a id="SAF-B0317"></a>
Rollback or disablement / closure. Rollback or disable when core controls fail. Close only after root-cause/uncertainty record, corrected configuration, required regressions, independent safety review, affected-scope assessment, truthful participant update and evidence disposition.

<a id="SAF-B0318"></a>
##### B — False claim of external action

<a id="SAF-B0319"></a>
Triage. Compare the claimed save/send/delete/call/grant with actual authorization, attempt and confirmation receipts. A statement that emergency help was contacted without confirmation is a critical safety failure; an uncertain receipt is not success.

<a id="SAF-B0320"></a>
Containment / owner / minimized evidence. Engineering/action owner disables the affected action/status renderer; safety lead handles rescue implications and security checks unauthorized execution. Reconcile side effects before retrying; do not double-send, duplicate grants or repeat a possible charge.

<a id="SAF-B0321"></a>
Participant communication / vendor / professional escalation. Correct the false claim promptly in a safe channel: say what is and is not confirmed, and direct to emergency help when needed. Preserve the selected claim and bounded receipts, not a full chat. Escalate to the actual provider of the faulty action; counsel/privacy review consequential disclosures or harm allegations.

<a id="SAF-B0322"></a>
Rollback or disablement / closure. Close only after accurate state reconciliation, repaired confirmation contract, duplicate/timeout tests, independent review and clear participant correction. Keep emergency dispatch and other unapproved actions disabled.

<a id="SAF-B0323"></a>
##### C — Crisis resource incorrect or unavailable

<a id="SAF-B0324"></a>
Triage. Distinguish wrong digits/destination from a website outage, unsupported device link or reported service delay. Do not ask a distressed participant to keep testing the number or wait for Steward support.

<a id="SAF-B0325"></a>
Containment / owner / minimized evidence. Resource owner disables an incorrect entry and promotes a verified fallback; engineering freezes unreviewed resource edits and checks tampering. If no safe core presentation remains, stop affected conversations. Keep verified static emergency guidance available.

<a id="SAF-B0326"></a>
Participant communication / vendor / professional escalation. Communicate the corrected contact and uncertainty without claiming successful connection. Store resource ID/bundle, observed failure and verification source; no disclosure narrative. Contact the resource organization through appropriate administrative channels or the platform vendor, not an emergency line for debugging. Privacy/security/counsel join if redirect or leakage occurred.

<a id="SAF-B0327"></a>
Rollback or disablement / closure. Close with official-source verification, registry review, device/accessibility/failure tests and updated bundle. A website returning does not prove telephone availability, and no uncoordinated emergency test call is part of closure.

<a id="SAF-B0328"></a>
##### D — Private information exposed

<a id="SAF-B0329"></a>
Triage. Treat credible cross-participant, public-link, log, support-tool or vendor exposure as SEV0/SEV1 pending scope assessment. Determine affected routes and authority, not the person’s sensitive life details.

<a id="SAF-B0330"></a>
Containment / owner / minimized evidence. Security/incident lead restricts endpoints, revokes exposed keys/links and blocks unauthorized access. Preserve a bounded evidence manifest and authorized samples; stop further content logging and isolate leaked stores. Do not erase relevant evidence blindly.

<a id="SAF-B0331"></a>
Participant communication / vendor / professional escalation. Privacy and counsel determine affected people, safe notification, legal clocks and valid holds. Support communicates confirmed facts without exposing additional data. Vendor manager initiates contractual incident/deletion obligations with minimum necessary details; no casual upload of the leak into vendor chat.

<a id="SAF-B0332"></a>
Rollback or disablement / closure. Close after access is contained, secrets rotated where necessary, scope/notification decisions recorded, copies addressed, isolation/log/restore tests pass and independent security review accepts recovery. Unknown scope remains an explicit unresolved issue.

<a id="SAF-B0333"></a>
##### E — Sponsor / church privacy breach

<a id="SAF-B0334"></a>
Triage. Determine whether an individual’s private content or prohibited named activity became available to a sponsor, church, leader, group or facilitator. Membership and sponsorship provide no private-record authority.

<a id="SAF-B0335"></a>
Containment / owner / minimized evidence. Security and privacy leads revoke the feed/export/role/link and freeze affected cohort reporting. Preserve minimal recipient/delivery references and selected evidence. Investigators with church/sponsor conflicts recuse; no investigation through the implicated leader.

<a id="SAF-B0336"></a>
Participant communication / vendor / professional escalation. Contact the participant through a safe approved channel and coordinate recipients/vendor containment without unnecessary re-disclosure. Counsel evaluates notification, preservation and appropriate requests to stop access/delete copies; do not promise retrieval of every recipient copy. Escalate the integration/reporting vendor if applicable.

<a id="SAF-B0337"></a>
Rollback or disablement / closure. Close only after role/report tests, independent privacy/security review, impact assessment, required communications and residual-copy status. Do not restore reporting merely because the sponsor apologizes.

<a id="SAF-B0338"></a>
##### F — Dangerous product bug

<a id="SAF-B0339"></a>
Triage. Classify the malfunction: unsafe guidance sequence, wrong participant context, bypassed authorization, corrupted approved wording, billing during distress or safety resources hidden by a quota/error. Assess affected versions and features.

<a id="SAF-B0340"></a>
Containment / owner / minimized evidence. Engineering containment operator disables the smallest demonstrably isolatable surface; otherwise stop the pilot. Safety/security lead assesses harm and data impact. Keep the original defect evidence minimal and reproduce synthetically; do not debug with a production database copy.

<a id="SAF-B0341"></a>
Participant communication / vendor / professional escalation. Support explains the actual limitation and safe alternatives without blaming the participant. Vendor manager escalates provider/infrastructure faults with metadata first; privacy/counsel assess any data or rights effect. Correct records only through reviewed repair preserving actual approval history.

<a id="SAF-B0342"></a>
Rollback or disablement / closure. Close after fix, concurrency/failure/authorization regressions, independent relevant review, safe rollback or forward-recovery proof and verified participant-facing state. A UI-only hide is not containment of a reachable backend.

<a id="SAF-B0343"></a>
##### G — Repeated concerning model pattern

<a id="SAF-B0344"></a>
Triage. Look for recurrence in synthetic tests, voluntarily selected reports and approved metadata, not routine private transcript scanning. Low complaint count does not prove safety; repeated borderline outputs can indicate a systemic failure.

<a id="SAF-B0345"></a>
Containment / owner / minimized evidence. Production safety lead opens a pattern incident, links cases without merging their private payloads and freezes rollout/enrollment where critical/high exposure is plausible. Engineering compares candidate/baseline versions and tests original synthetic variants.

<a id="SAF-B0346"></a>
Participant communication / vendor / professional escalation. Provide affected participants case-specific updates when justified; do not announce another person’s disclosure to the cohort. Escalate model/provider behavior through vendor management under approved evidence limits. Privacy/counsel review any broader analysis or required notice; O10 still forbids private-content evaluation reuse.

<a id="SAF-B0347"></a>
Rollback or disablement / closure. Close only with scoped causal hypotheses, counterexample testing, reviewed prompt/model/control changes and independent evidence of remediation. “Newer model installed” or a better average score is insufficient.

<a id="SAF-B0348"></a>
##### H — Participant complaint alleging harm

<a id="SAF-B0349"></a>
Triage. Acknowledge respectfully; distinguish immediate danger, an allegation and verified facts. Do not debate credibility, diagnose, retaliate, offer hush terms or imply that using Steward waived rights. Give emergency resources where current danger is reported.

<a id="SAF-B0350"></a>
Containment / owner / minimized evidence. Independent incident owner coordinates safety, privacy and counsel; recuse any implicated owner/staff member. Preserve only potentially relevant evidence under counsel’s scoped instructions while continuing unrelated eligible rights/deletion work. Contain credible dangerous functionality before causation is established.

<a id="SAF-B0351"></a>
Participant communication / vendor / professional escalation. Support uses a safe channel, clear case ID and realistic next update. Counsel guides appropriate communications and legal/vendor/insurer involvement; vendor escalation shares minimum necessary evidence under authority. No full private conversation dump or unsupported admission/denial of causation.

<a id="SAF-B0352"></a>
Rollback or disablement / closure. Closure documents investigation, verified facts and uncertainty, remedial action/retests, required notices, participant response or appeal route where applicable, access removal and hold/disposition decisions. Unresolved serious risk remains release-blocking even if the complaint is withdrawn.
